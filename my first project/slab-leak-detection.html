What our Slab Leak Detection service includes

Every detection visit covers the full diagnostic sequence, not just a quick listen. You get the isolation test, the acoustic and thermal scan, a marked location, and a written summary.

Work order

Slab Leak Detection
  • Full system isolation: We shut the main and pressurize the hot and cold lines separately with a pressure testing gauge. This tells us which side is losing water and rules out fixtures, irrigation, and the water heater before we look at the slab.
  • Acoustic and thermal scan: An acoustic leak detection microphone picks up the sound of escaping water, and a thermal imaging camera shows temperature bands on the floor. Together they narrow the break to a small zone we can mark with tape.
  • Marked location and photos: We tape the floor and photograph the scan results so you can see exactly where the problem is. You keep the images for your records, insurance, or a second opinion if you want one before any concrete is cut.
  • Repair and pressure test: If you approve the repair, we cut a minimal access opening, replace the failed section with new copper or PEX tubing, seal the slab, and hold pressure for 30 minutes to confirm the fix. One crew handles detection and repair.
  • Written findings summary: You get a short written summary of what failed, where it was, what we replaced, and what to watch for.

What Slab Leak Detection in Greer actually involves

A slab leak never gets smaller. Water keeps moving through the soil, the damage spreads, and the repair grows with it. Finding the break early keeps the fix small and the cost down.

Slab Leak Detection in Greer is the process of locating water escaping from a pressurized supply line or drain line buried beneath your concrete foundation. We start by isolating the system, then use an acoustic leak detection microphone to listen for the hiss of water pushing through soil or a cracked pipe. A thermal imaging camera confirms temperature shifts, and a pressure testing gauge tells us whether the loss is on the hot side, the cold side, or both. Once we know the line, we mark the floor and plan the least invasive repair.

Greer homes sit on clay-heavy soil that swells after heavy rain and shrinks in summer drought, and that movement stresses copper pipe and older PVC pipe until joints fail. Homes built before 1990 often have copper under the slab, while newer builds use PEX tubing that can still rub through at fittings. How our slab leak detection process works

Every job follows the same sequence: confirm there is a leak, locate it, explain what we found, repair it, and prove the line holds.

  1. Phone triage

    We ask about your water bill, the sound of running water when nothing is on, warm spots on the floor, and whether the meter moves with the main shut off.

  2. System isolation

    On arrival we shut the main, pressurize the system in sections, and watch a pressure testing gauge for drop-off.

  3. Acoustic and thermal scan

    We walk the floor with an acoustic leak detection microphone and a thermal imaging camera, listening for the signature hiss and watching for temperature bands.

  4. Confirm and mark

    A pipe inspection camera or tracer gas leak detector confirms the exact line and depth.

  5. Repair and pressure test

    We cut a minimal access opening, repair or reroute the failed section with new copper or PEX, and seal the slab.

Tools and methods we use on every job

Each method answers a different question. Together they leave very little to guess.

Listens for the high-frequency hiss of pressurized water escaping a pipe. We walk the floor in a grid and compare signal strength to narrow the break to a small zone.

Shows temperature differences on the floor surface. A hot line leak reads as a warm band, and a cold line leak reads as a cool one, which tells us which side to chase.

Confirms there is a leak and which section it is on. We pressurize the hot and cold lines separately and watch for pressure drop over 20 to 30 minutes.

Lets us look inside a drain line to spot root intrusion, cracks, or offset joints that acoustic tools cannot confirm. It also verifies depth before we cut.

Used when acoustic signals are ambiguous. We introduce a safe tracer gas into the line and follow the concentration to the break, which is useful on deep or noisy slabs.

Problems we solve for Greer homeowners

A hidden slab leak shows up in ways that do not always point straight to the pipe.

Ask about yours

  • Water bill doubled with no change in use

    A pinhole leak can waste 10,000 gallons a month. Left alone, you pay hundreds extra and the water keeps eroding soil under the slab.

  • Warm spot on the floor over a hot line

    A hot water leak heats the slab and can warp laminate or tile within weeks. The moisture also feeds mold under carpet and baseboards.

  • Sound of running water when everything is off

    That hiss is pressurized water escaping underground. It will not stop on its own and often signals a joint that is about to fail completely.

  • Cracks in tile and sticking doors

    Water softens the soil under the slab and lets it settle unevenly. Cosmetic cracks can become structural movement if the leak runs for months.
