What Our Pool Leak Detection Service Includes

Every detection visit covers the full pool system: shell, fittings, skimmer, main drain, return lines, suction lines, and the equipment pad.

Work order

Pool Leak Detection
  • Bucket Test and Water Line Marking: We mark the pool water line and set a bucket test to separate evaporation from a real leak. This takes 24 hours and gives you a baseline number you can check against after the repair.
  • Full Pressure Test of All Lines: Returns, skimmer, and main drain each get plugged and charged with air. We record the pressure on a gauge and watch for drop-off, which tells us exactly which line is losing water.
  • Acoustic and Thermal Scanning: We walk the pool perimeter and equipment pad with an acoustic microphone and thermal camera, listening for the leak signature and looking for temperature differences under the deck.
  • Dye Testing at Fittings and Shell: Leak detection dye goes around the skimmer throat, light niche, return fittings, and main drain. If dye pulls into a crack or gap, we have found the leak and can often patch it the same visit.
  • Written Findings With Photos: You get a summary of what we tested, what we found, and where it is. Photos and a marked diagram show the leak location, and the repair price is written on the same page.

How Our Pool Leak Detection Process Runs

Every job follows the same five steps, whether it is a vinyl pool in Greer Station or a commercial pool off Highway 14.

  1. Bucket Test

    We fill a bucket, set it on the first step, and mark both the bucket and pool water lines.

  2. Pressure Testing

    We plug the returns, skimmer, and main drain, then charge each line with air using a pressure testing gauge. A line that will not hold pressure has a break.

  3. Acoustic and Thermal Scan

    An acoustic leak detection microphone picks up the hiss of escaping water, and a thermal imaging camera shows temperature differences where water is moving under the deck.

  4. Dye and Camera Confirmation

    We inject leak detection dye around the skimmer throat, light niche, return fittings, and main drain. If dye pulls into a crack, that is your leak.

  5. Repair Plan and Fix

    You get a written summary with photos, the leak location marked on a diagram, and a repair price. Most shell leaks are patched with epoxy resin the same visit.
